Education for the world outside the classroom

Academic education gives young people an essential foundation.

Legal Literacy

BOX 1 — LEGAL LITERACY

Legal Literacy

Know your rights. Understand your responsibilities. Know where to turn.

Law affects people long before they ever enter a courtroom.

It influences what happens when someone is bullied, defrauded, harassed or exploited. It also shapes consumer protections, children’s rights, online responsibilities, access to public institutions and the remedies available when something goes wrong.

Our sessions can explore:

* Child rights
* Consumer rights
* Cybercrime
* Consent and personal safety
* Basic contracts
* Reporting wrongdoing
* Approaching the police
* Legal aid and accessing help

Our aim: to help young people understand that rights exist, responsibilities matter and help is available when something goes wrong.

⸻

BOX 2 — FINANCIAL STUDIES

Financial Studies

Financial confidence begins with small decisions.

Money becomes part of our lives long before most of us begin earning it.

Young people already encounter choices involving saving, spending, digital payments, advertisements, online purchases and financial scams.

Our sessions can explore:

* Needs versus wants
* Saving
* Simple budgeting
* Banks and bank accounts
* UPI and digital payments
* Financial fraud and scams
* Responsible spending
* Basic financial planning

Our aim: to help students make informed choices with the resources they have.

Responsible financial behaviour does not begin with a large salary.

It can begin with the first ₹100 someone decides whether to save or spend.

⸻

BOX 3 — DIGITAL LITERACY

Digital Literacy

Knowing how to use technology is not the same as knowing how to navigate it.

For many young people, the internet is part of everyday life.

It is where they communicate, learn, entertain themselves, discover information and increasingly interact with the wider world.

Our sessions can explore:

* Strong passwords
* Two-factor authentication
* Phishing and online scams
* Privacy
* Cyberbullying
* Misinformation
* Digital footprints
* Responsible social-media use
* Online communication
* Basic productivity tools

Give this box a small visual emphasis to the phrase:

Pause. Question. Then click.

Our aim: to help young people use technology with confidence, awareness and responsibility.

⸻

BOX 4 — ENVIRONMENTAL EDUCATION

Environmental Education

Global problems often begin with local choices.

Climate change, pollution, biodiversity loss and waste can feel like enormous global problems.

But environmental responsibility often begins much closer to home.

The water we use.

The waste we create.

The resources we consume.

The environment surrounding our own communities.

Our sessions can explore:

* Waste management
* Water conservation
* Pollution
* Biodiversity
* Climate change
* Responsible consumption
* Local environmental action

Our aim: to help students understand both the problem and their role within it.

We want environmental education to leave students feeling capable, rather than helpless.

OUR APPROACH

Keep the existing Our Approach section after “Why These Four?” exactly as a separate section.

Use:

Our Approach

Less lecturing. More learning.

We believe practical education works best when students are involved in it.

Our sessions are designed around five principles:

Practical
We connect concepts with situations students may actually encounter.

Interactive
Activities, conversations, scenarios and questions matter more than passive listening.

Accessible
Complex ideas are broken down into language students can understand.

Relevant
We focus on problems and decisions that exist in students’ everyday lives.

Action-oriented
Students should leave knowing not only what something means, but what they can actually do with that knowledge.

Most importantly, we want the classroom to feel like a place where asking questions is encouraged.

Because sometimes the most valuable lesson begins with:

“But why?”
